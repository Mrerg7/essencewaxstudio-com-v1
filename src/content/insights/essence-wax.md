Wax epilation extracts hair from the root using warm or hard wax, providing smooth skin for 3 to 4 weeks. Hard wax is preferred for sensitive areas because it adheres to hair, not skin.

## What the method actually does

The wax grips the hair shaft and pulls the follicle clean. Because the root comes out — not just the visible shaft — regrowth starts as a fine, soft tip rather than a blunt stubble. That is the difference people feel between waxing and shaving, and it is why clients return on a 3 to 4 week rhythm.

Consistency compounds. With regular sessions the follicle is disturbed on the same cycle each time, and many people notice regrowth becoming progressively finer.

## Why hard wax, not strip wax

Soft (strip) wax is spread thin and lifted with paper or cloth. It works well on large, flat areas like legs. On the bikini line and underarms it is less forgiving: it clings to live skin as much as hair, which is where the sting comes from.

Hard wax sets on its own. Once it cools it has bonded to the hair, so removal pulls at the follicle instead of tugging the surface. For a private studio built around Brazilian work, hard wax is the default, not the upgrade.

## Preparation is half the result

Hair needs to be roughly ¼ to ½ inch — about two to three weeks of growth for most people. Too short and the wax has nothing to grip; too long and the first pull is sharper than it needs to be.

Exfoliate the day *before*, not the day of. Avoid retinoids, acids, and self-tanner for 48 hours. Clean, dry skin grips better than lotioned skin.

## After the pull

Temporary redness is normal and typically settles within hours. Gentle exfoliation around day three keeps ingrowns from setting up, and loose clothing for the first day gives freshly opened follicles room to close.
